>1. Have a thought.

>    1. Know that you’ll need to transform this note into something else, if it’s to become useful.

>[!quote]+ The main note has a title that tells you about the idea found in the note. It has an alphanumeric ID for easy indexing and retrieval. It has links to other ideas already stored in the zettelkasten (here, shown with single brackets), with reasons given for why the connections were made.


>[!quote]+ Note-taking allows you to quickly get ideas out of your head and onto the page, forcing previously amorphous thoughts to engage with the language needed to convert them into coherent ideas.

>[!quote]+ Struggling to put an idea into words forces us to wrestle with the language through which that idea will be expressed. Rather than being a means of recording what we already believe, writing, even in the form of short notes, helps us understand what we think we know.

>[!quote]+ Note-taking is writing, and writing is a path toward knowing.

>[!quote]+ Some of the best ideas come from the least academic sources.

>[!quote]+ In its most basic form, a main note should have at least two components: a single idea, and a link to another idea stored in your zettelkasten.
